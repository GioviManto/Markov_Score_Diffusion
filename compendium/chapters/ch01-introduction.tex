\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:intro}

\section{Topic and motivation}

Generative diffusion models have become the dominant paradigm for
synthesising high-dimensional data: they power state-of-the-art systems
for images, audio, video, and molecular structures \citep{sohl2015deep,
ho2020denoising, song2021scorebased, karras2022elucidating}. The same
construction underlies the image and video generators deployed at scale by the
large technology companies, from latent diffusion \citep{rombach2022latent}
onwards, and reaches beyond media: structure prediction in AlphaFold~3 places
atomic coordinates with a diffusion module \citep{abramson2024alphafold3}. They were introduced not simply as an engineering construction, but through the language of \emph{non-equilibrium} thermodynamics: a forward process progressively destroys the structure of the data distribution, while a learned reverse process attempts to reconstruct it, with the corresponding path measures connected through ideas from fluctuation theorems for driven systems \citep{sohl2015deep}.
Their
central object is the \emph{score function}, the gradient of the
log-density of progressively noised data,
\begin{equation}
  \score(x, t) \;=\; \nabla_x \log p_t(x),
  \label{eq:intro-score}
\end{equation}

which is the one object a model must learn in order to reverse a noising
process: given the forward process, its schedule and its terminal law, the score
determines the reverse drift \citep{anderson1982reverse}. In practice the score is approximated by a neural network trained by denoising
score matching \citep{hyvarinen2005estimation, vincent2011connection}, and
what that training targets depends on which objective is meant. At the
population level the unrestricted denoising objective is minimised by the
score of the noised data law itself; conditional on a finite training set,
the empirical objective is minimised by the score of the \emph{empirical}
distribution: a sum of point masses at the training samples, each blurred by
the forward kernel. A network that reached that empirical minimiser and was
integrated
back to zero noise would reproduce its training set and nothing else; that
diffusion models instead generate new data means the trained score departs from
the minimiser of its own finite-sample objective, in a way that is still only
partly charted
(Chapter~\ref{ch:statmech}, Section~\ref{sec:sm-three-scores}). Deciding what a
trained network has actually learned therefore requires holding it against the
score it \emph{should} have produced --- and for essentially every distribution
of interest that reference is unavailable, so a network's error cannot be
separated from the difficulty of the target it was chasing
\citep{kadkhodaie2024generalization}.

A complementary programme, initiated in the statistical-physics community,
replaces the black box with \emph{exactly solvable models}: data distributions
simple enough that the score is available in closed form, yet rich enough to
show the phenomena seen in real systems --- speciation, memorisation, and
dynamical phase transitions along the generation trajectory
\citep{biroli2023generative, biroli2024dynamical, raya2023spontaneous,
ghio2024sampling, achilli2026speciation}.

This thesis works inside that programme, on a family chosen so that the
reference score is both computable and nonlinear. The data are a trajectory
$a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{\nsites-1}$ of a first-order Markov process on $\R^{\nsites}$
with continuous, non-Gaussian transitions, variance-matched so that competing
innovation laws differ only beyond the second moment. Coordinatewise
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noising multiplies the prior by unary factors and adds no
edges among the latent variables, so the posterior factor graph is still the
prior's chain --- a tree, on which sum--product is exact. The joint score of the
whole noisy sequence is then available as an exact functional recursion for a
correlated, non-Gaussian model, and numerically to a validated tolerance once
the continuous messages are discretised (Figure~\ref{fig:thesis-pipeline}).
Later chapters drop the assumption that the chain's parameters are known and
recover them from noised sequences alone.

Sequential data, such as videos, often exhibit strong local dependence: a frame is
typically much more constrained by nearby frames than by distant ones. Modern
architectures already exploit related forms of locality. Convolutional networks, and
in particular the U-Net architectures commonly used in diffusion models, build in
local receptive fields, weight sharing, and multiscale composition
\citep{krizhevsky2012imagenet,lecun2015deep}. They therefore should not be viewed
as structure-agnostic function approximators.

What remains less explicit is the probabilistic structure of the data. If the clean
sequence is Markovian, its joint law admits a precise local factorisation, and Bayesian
denoising can exploit this structure directly through message passing. This raises the
central question studied here: \emph{how much of the Markov structure of the clean
dynamics remains visible after diffusion noising, how is it represented in the score,
and to what extent can a learned score recover and exploit it from finite data?}
The posterior representation of the score provides a convenient way to study this
question. When the Markov prior is known, the corresponding posterior quantities can
be computed explicitly by belief propagation, providing a structured reference against
which learned score estimators can be compared. The aim is therefore not to argue that
neural score models ignore structure, but to understand how much structure they learn
implicitly, how noising transforms it, and what is gained by making that structure
explicit.

Two classical theories meet the modern one. For Gaussian Markov chains, the score is governed by structured matrix algebra; more generally, the score-to-posterior identity turns denoising on a chain into inference on a tree, where BP and Kalman filtering apply \citep{pearl1988probabilistic,mezard2009information,kalman1960new,donoho2009message}. This thesis develops these two views, shows where they agree, and studies what is lost when the underlying structure is only approximated.


\section{Positioning and gap}

Chapter~\ref{ch:statmech} reviews the literature this work builds on; the gap
it leaves is stated here.

The statistical physics of generative diffusion has
produced closed-form analyses of the backward dynamics and their transitions
\citep{biroli2023generative, biroli2024dynamical, achilli2026speciation,
sclocchi2025phase, bonnaire2025memorize}. Exact posterior inference on
Gauss--Markov chains is classical, through the Kalman filter and smoother and
their formulation as sum--product message passing
\citep{kalman1960new, rauch1965maximum, kschischang2001factor,
mezard2009information}. And message passing with continuous variables closes
its messages inside a parametric family, which is the step connecting belief
propagation to AMP and to the TAP equations
\citep{thouless1977solution, donoho2009message, zdeborova2016statistical}.

The solvable-model analyses cited above treat data as
unstructured collections of samples: the only time axis they carry is the
diffusion time, and the \emph{internal} time of a sequence, a frame correlated
with its neighbours by a dynamical rule, does not appear in that line of
work. The inference
literature has the machinery for that internal time but does not ask how the exact posterior, and
so the exact score, deforms as the noise level is swept from zero to
saturation. And the standard justification for closing messages in a Gaussian
family is a central-limit argument that requires each node to receive many
messages; on a chain each node receives exactly two, so the argument fails by
construction.

The gap this thesis addresses is: the joint score of a noised
Markov sequence, computed exactly where that is possible, approximated in a
controlled and measured way where it is not, and learned from noised data when
the dynamics are unknown.

Where no closed form exists, the reference used throughout is
deterministic quadrature and not Monte Carlo. A particle smoother carries
sampling error, which would add a second error term to every comparison and
force the cost of an approximation and the cost of the reference to be
disentangled before either could be read. Grid-based inference carries a
discretisation error instead, which can be bounded separately and reported,
and that is what makes it usable as a reference.

\section{Research questions}\label{sec:rq}

The thesis addresses six questions. The first four concern scores under
\emph{known} dynamics and the last two concern learning dynamics that are
unknown.

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1.}] For a stationary Gaussian AR(1) chain observed
  through coordinatewise Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise, what is the joint
  score, and how do its spatial couplings vary with the diffusion time?

  \item[\textbf{RQ2.}] How can the same Gaussian-chain score be computed
  by belief propagation?

  \item[\textbf{RQ3.}] For selected non-Gaussian innovation models, how
  accurate is a Gaussian-message approximation relative to a validated
  numerical belief-propagation reference, over the parameter ranges
  studied?

  \item[\textbf{RQ4.}] In the Gaussian model, how rapidly does the
  influence of remote observations on the score decay, and what limited
  guidance does this provide for local approximations of the score?

  \item[\textbf{RQ5.}] Under what assumptions can an \emph{unknown} local
  transition law be recovered from noised sequences alone, and which
  numerical, optimisation and statistical checks are required before such
  a recovery claim is reliable?

  \item[\textbf{RQ6.}] On a family where the Markov assumption holds
  exactly, what is supplying that assumption worth, measured against
  denoising-score-matching networks trained on identical data and given
  none of it?
\end{itemize}

These questions are deliberately confined to the models named in them.
No claim is made about general Markov processes, about universal
properties of learned diffusion scores, or about practical neural
architectures. In particular RQ6 is a question about the value of a
correct structural assumption on one family, not a comparison of learning
algorithms in general: the two arms are asymmetric in supplied
information by construction, which is what makes the measurement
meaningful and also what bounds it.

Chapter~\ref{ch:ring} answers no numbered question. It comes first because it
is where the work began, and because what it settles motivates the six: on a
rotating-ring model, whose state space and estimand both differ from the
chain's, it shows what joint observations can identify that no single-frame
marginal can.

\section{Contributions}

\noindent Three contributions, in the order the body develops them.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A Gaussian case worked out completely.} For the stationary
  AR(1) chain under coordinatewise OU corruption, we derive the score in
  closed form, recover the classical identification of belief propagation on
  it with Kalman filtering followed by RTS smoothing, and quantify what
  ignoring distant evidence costs; the closed-form influence-decay rate is
  derived where it is used, in
  Chapter~\ref{ch:em-results} (Chapters~\ref{ch:gaussian}
  and~\ref{ch:gaussian-bp}).

  \item \textbf{What belief propagation still gets right off the Gaussian
  case, and what it gives up.} The message-passing recursion stays exact for
  any innovation law; it is the closure of its messages into a finite
  parametric family that Gaussianity buys, and Laplace innovations lose. We
  show that projecting the exact message onto the Gaussian family is not an
  approximation to shrug off but a plain object, the LMMSE estimator of the
  covariance-matched Gaussian model (Theorem~\ref{thm:l-lmmse}), so the gap it
  leaves is attributable to the choice of family rather than to any one
  implementation (Chapter~\ref{ch:laplace}).

  \item \textbf{Learning the chain itself.} Fisher's identity lets us fit an
  unknown transition kernel from noised sequences by EM, each E-step and
  likelihood gradient supplied by one forward--backward pass at the current
  parameter value, with no derivative taken through the recursion
  (Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method}). Chapters~\ref{ch:em-kernel}
  and~\ref{ch:em-results} then measure what the recovered structure is worth
  against denoising score matching, and where that advantage runs out.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Structure of the thesis}

Chapter~\ref{ch:statmech} is the context: statistical mechanics and the
variational principle behind the Boltzmann distribution, energy-based models as
the same object with a learned energy, generative diffusion and the reverse-time
SDE, and then the distinction the rest of the thesis turns on, between the
exact, the empirical and the fitted score. It closes with the reading of U-Nets
as belief propagation that makes exact inference a serious alternative to a
trained network rather than a toy one. Chapter~\ref{ch:model} fixes the model
and the estimand: the Markov sequence, the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck channel, the
argument that the joint score and not a per-frame score is the object worth
studying, and the factor-graph language the computations are written in.

Chapter~\ref{ch:ring} begins the research where the research began, with a
planar trajectory near a circle. It shows that the rotation rate carries zero
Fisher information in every one-frame marginal and positive information in the
trajectory. That is the argument for the joint score in a single exact
statement, and it is what sends the following chapters to a model where the
joint score can be computed in full.

Chapters~\ref{ch:gaussian} and~\ref{ch:gaussian-bp} do that for the stationary
Gaussian chain, first with matrices and then with messages: the score in closed
form, the lifecycle of its couplings, the identification of belief propagation
with the Kalman filter and the Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoother, and the
measured price of locality; the closed-form geometric decay behind it is
derived in Chapter~\ref{ch:em-results}, where it drives a design argument
(RQ1, RQ2, RQ4). Chapter~\ref{ch:laplace} leaves Gaussianity in the most
controlled way available, through Laplace innovations, builds a validated
numerical reference, and measures exactly what a Gaussian message family
discards (RQ3).

Chapters~\ref{ch:em-method} and~\ref{ch:em-kernel} drop the assumption that the
transition law is known and recover it from noised sequences alone, first two
parameters and then a whole kernel, with the convergence schedule of the two
coordinates (RQ5). Chapter~\ref{ch:em-results}
measures what supplying the Markov structure is worth against networks trained
on identical data, and locates where it stops being worth anything (RQ6).
Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions} answers the six questions and states what is left
open.
