\chapter{How the Reported Ratios Were Aggregated}\label{app:aggregation-chapter}
\label{app:aggregation}

A ratio is a nonlinear function of two means, so it depends on the order in
which the averaging is done: $\E[X]/\E[Y]$ is not $\E[X/Y]$, and the gap
between them grows with the spread. Both ratios reported in this thesis
therefore need the same question asked of them, namely whether the number is a
property of the estimator or of the estimand chosen to summarise it.

Both were recomputed under four alternative aggregations of the same frozen
cells, refitting nothing. The outcome, in one sentence each. For the
unstructured comparison the direction is the same under every aggregation; the
reported estimand is the schedule-pooled paired per-seed ratio that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:em-pooling} defines, and the mean of per-level ratios --- which
earlier drafts reported --- is systematically the \emph{largest} of the
alternatives ($\ratiocellwiselo$--$\ratiocellwisehi\times$ against the pooled
$\ratiolo$--$\ratiohi\times$), which is why naming the estimand matters. For
the
structure-aware comparison the reported estimand is \emph{not} uniformly the
largest, but the floor across all four aggregations is
$\structaggfloor\times$ with a spread of $\structaggspread$, so the reading of
a low single-digit multiple at the smallest training set growing to roughly six
at the largest survives every one of them.

Both recomputations are reproduced in full below, so that the robustness
claim can be checked against the numbers without leaving this document. They
are generated by
\codefile{tools/make\_aggregation\_robustness.py} and
\codefile{tools/make\_aggregation\_structured.py} from the same frozen outputs
that produce the headline table, so the two cannot drift apart; the pooled
column of the first reproduces Table~\ref{tab:pointwise} exactly, which is the
consistency check that the alternatives are recomputations of the same cells
and not of a different experiment. The same tables, with the longer protocol
discussion around them, also appear in \compendium{}.
