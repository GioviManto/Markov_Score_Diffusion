\chapter{What Structure Buys, and Where It Stops Paying}
\label{ch:em-results}

Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method} built an estimator exact in its inference step and
approximate only in its numerical representation and its budget;
Chapter~\ref{ch:em-kernel} measured what it costs to run. This chapter asks
what it is \emph{worth}, relative to networks trained on the same clean
trajectories and given none of the structure: how much supplying the chain
reduces denoising error at a fixed number of clean trajectories, how much of
that survives against a baseline given locality but not the model, how much
mixture capacity the estimator needs, and how far the Markov assumption can
be wrong before the structure stops paying.

\section{What is compared, and how it is scored}\label{sec:em-estimand}

Fix a diffusion time $t$ and a test set $\{x^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{J}$ of noised
sequences from the model of Section~\ref{sec:em-model}, and let
$\score^{\star}$ be the exact joint score, supplied by the validated grid
reference under the true kernel. For an estimator $\widehat\score$ the
relative $L^2$ score error is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}\big[\widehat{\score}; t\big]
  \;=\;
  \Bigg(
  \frac{\sum_{j}\norm{\widehat{\score}(x^{(j)},t) - \score^{\star}(x^{(j)},t)}^2}
       {\sum_{j}\norm{\score^{\star}(x^{(j)},t)}^2}
  \Bigg)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:em-relerr}
\end{equation}
scale free, with $0.1$ meaning a tenth of the signal's own size. Pooling over
the noise schedule $\mathcal T$ pools the two sums, not the ratios,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}^2\big[\widehat{\score}\big]
  = \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}} w_t \,\mathcal{E}^2\big[\widehat{\score}; t\big],
  \qquad
  w_t =
  \frac{\E\norm{\score^{\star}(X_t,t)}^2}{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{T}}\E\norm{\score^{\star}(X_s,s)}^2},
  \label{eq:em-pooling}
\end{equation}
the weights being the measured reference score energies of the frozen test
bundle. The covariance-matched Gaussian chain explains their shape: there
$\E\|\score^\star\|^2 = \operatorname{tr}\Sigt^{-1}$, which falls from
$\operatorname{tr}\Qz = 193.4$ at $t \to 0$ to $\nsites = 32$ as
$t \to \infty$ with no $1/t$ singularity, so the schedule weights its
lower-noise levels most heavily; the four smallest of the twelve levels carry
$54.5\%$ of the measured weight (Table~\ref{tab:em-locality}).

The two arms are the EM--BP estimator, given the Markov factorisation, and a
denoising-score-matching network trained by \eqref{eq:sm-dsm} with no
structural information. Both are fitted from the same $\nseq$ clean
trajectories, and that, not ``the same data'', is the equality enforced.
What each arm sees of those trajectories differs, and the difference runs
\emph{against} the result reported here: EM--BP sees one frozen noised
realisation of each trajectory and never the clean signal, whereas the
network is trained on paired $(a,x)$ data as \eqref{eq:sm-dsm} requires and
redraws $t$ and the corrupting noise at every gradient step. Both asymmetries
are deliberate, the baseline being the standard training recipe for its
class. The reported quantity is
\begin{equation}
  R \;=\; \frac{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{base}}}{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EM}}},
  \qquad R > 1 \ \text{meaning the structured estimator is more accurate,}
  \label{eq:em-ratio}
\end{equation}
formed cell by cell, a cell being one (training-set size, seed) pair with both
arms refitted: a statement about value per clean trajectory under the two
standard recipes, not about data efficiency at a matched information
interface. Because $R$ is a ratio of the quantities \eqref{eq:em-relerr}, the
aggregation matters: squared error and reference energy are summed over the
twelve levels before the square root, per seed, the ratio is formed within
the seed, and the reported number is the mean of the sixteen paired per-seed
ratios with its standard error, the seed being the inferential unit.
Appendix~\ref{app:repro} recomputes the same cells under alternative
summaries: the direction and order of magnitude are the same in all, but the
mean of per-level ratios, which earlier drafts reported, is systematically
the largest ($\ratiocellwiselo$--$\ratiocellwisehi\times$ against the pooled
$\ratiolo$--$\ratiohi\times$). Table~\ref{tab:em-protocol} fixes the protocol
shared by Sections~\ref{sec:em-pointwise}--\ref{sec:em-capacity};
Section~\ref{sec:em-nonmarkov} changes the prior and
Section~\ref{sec:em-shape-information} is a likelihood calculation.

\section{The estimators}\label{sec:em-estimators}\label{sec:em-archs}

Every network arm is read as a score. Predicting the noise or the clean
signal are two parameterisations of one object,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\score}_\varepsilon(x,t) = -\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon}_\phi(x,t)}{\sqrt{\Deltat}},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\score}_{a}(x,t) = \frac{e^{-t}\,\widehat{a}_\phi(x,t) - x}{\Deltat},
  \label{eq:em-parameterisation}
\end{equation}
identical as function classes but different as optimisation problems, since
the substitution rescales the residual by $e^{t}\sqrt{\Deltat}$ and reweights
the noise levels; both are offered to every arm and chosen on validation, and
the screen below settles it in favour of the noise head within every
architecture. Throughout, $\tau(t) = (t, e^{-t}, \sqrt{\Deltat})$ is a time
embedding broadcast to every site.

\emph{EM--BP} fits the mixture kernel \eqref{eq:em-mixture-kernel} by
Section~\ref{sec:em-algorithm} and evaluates the score through the
score--posterior identity, with $3C = 24$ free parameters independent of
$\nsites$; it is supplied the Markov factorisation, the
linear-plus-innovation form of the kernel and homogeneity across sites, and
estimates the innovation law and $\corr$. \emph{The unstructured denoiser} is
a fully connected $\tanh$ network mapping the whole sequence and $\tau(t)$ to
the whole output,
\begin{equation}
  f_\phi(x,t) \;=\; W_3\,\tanh\!\big(W_2\,\tanh(W_1 [x;\tau(t)] + b_1) + b_2\big) + b_3,
  \label{eq:em-mlp}
\end{equation}
hidden width $128$, $25{,}248$ parameters, a thousand times EM--BP's; nothing
in it knows the sites are ordered, since permuting the coordinates of $x$ and
of the output is absorbed by permuting columns of $W_1$ and rows of $W_3$.
Because a large part of any margin over \eqref{eq:em-mlp} could be
permutation-blindness rather than the Markov model, three architectures that
encode sequence structure are screened. The \emph{weight-shared window head}
of radius $r$ applies one two-layer $\tanh$ predictor $g_\phi$ of width $W$
at every site to its window,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{y}_k \;=\; g_\phi\big(x_{k-r},\dots,x_{k+r};\ \tau(t)\big),
  \qquad k = 1,\dots,\nsites,
  \label{eq:em-window}
\end{equation}
a one-dimensional convolution of width $2r+1$ with pointwise layers, with a
parameter count $(2r+4)W + W^2 + 3W + 1$ independent of $\nsites$ and a
receptive radius fixed at $r$. The \emph{dilated convolutional stack} is a
residual stack of three-tap convolutions at dilations $d_\ell \in \{1,2,4,8\}$,
\begin{equation}
  h^{(\ell)}_k \;=\; h^{(\ell-1)}_k
  \;+\; P_\ell \tanh\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_{s \in \{-d_\ell,\,0,\,d_\ell\}} W_{\ell,s}\, h^{(\ell-1)}_{k+s} + b_\ell\Big),
  \label{eq:em-dilated}
\end{equation}
with receptive radius $15$, spanning the chain. The \emph{bidirectional
message-passing} network runs forward and backward recurrences with shared
local updates and a joint readout,
\begin{equation}
  \overrightarrow{h}_k = \tanh\!\big(A z_k + F \overrightarrow{h}_{k-1} + a\big),
  \quad
  \overleftarrow{h}_k = \tanh\!\big(B z_k + G \overleftarrow{h}_{k+1} + b\big),
  \quad
  \widehat{y}_k = C\big[\overrightarrow{h}_k; \overleftarrow{h}_k; z_k\big] + c,
  \label{eq:em-bimp}
\end{equation}
with $z_k = [x_k;\tau(t)]$: the topology of the two-sweep sum--product
schedule with affine-plus-$\tanh$ updates in place of the exact ones, and the
best claim of the three to being able to \emph{learn} what belief
propagation computes. Table~\ref{tab:em-architectures} sets the five arms
side by side. All network arms are trained with Adam at batch size $64$,
gradient clipping at $1$, a shared learning-rate grid and a shared checkpoint
ladder from $500$ to $64{,}000$ steps.

\section{Pointwise data efficiency}\label{sec:em-pointwise}

Against the unstructured denoiser, the structured estimator attains lower
relative score error by a factor of $\ratiolo$--$\ratiohi$ across the
\nsizesused{} training-set sizes (Table~\ref{tab:pointwise},
Figure~\ref{fig:em-value-of-structure}a), and is more accurate in all but
$\nreversed$ of the $\ncellsused$ scored (seed, size, level) cells. Every
tunable quantity is chosen on a validation bundle disjoint from training and
test, with the same privilege for both arms: the network selects its output
head and training length, EM--BP its iteration count, and that choice
coincides with the test-set optimum in $\netagree\%$ of cells for the network
and $\emagree\%$ for EM--BP. If each arm's error follows
$\mathcal{E} \approx c\,\nseq^{-\varrho}$ over the tested range, then
\begin{equation}
  R(\nseq) \;=\; \frac{c_{\mathrm{base}}}{c_{\mathrm{EM}}}\,
  \nseq^{\,\varrho_{\mathrm{EM}} - \varrho_{\mathrm{base}}},
  \label{eq:em-rate-ratio}
\end{equation}
so the ratio grows precisely when the structured arm converges faster in
data. The fitted finite-range slopes are
$\varrho_{\mathrm{EM}} = \pooledslopeem \pm \pooledslopeemse$ and
$\varrho_{\mathrm{base}} = \pooledslopebase \pm \pooledslopebasese$
(descriptive, not asymptotic), predicting that a $128$-fold increase in data
multiplies the ratio by $\pooledgrowthpred$; the measured ratio moves from
$\ratiofirst$ to $\ratiolast$, a factor of $\ratiogrowth$, non-monotonically.
Neither slope is the parametric $1/2$: EM--BP's mixture is misspecified
against a Laplace innovation and its grid is finite, and the network's
function class is unrestricted. Equation \eqref{eq:em-rate-ratio} describes
eight points and identifies no mechanism.

\section{How much of the margin is the architecture?}\label{sec:em-architecture}

\subsection{The bulk posterior in closed form}\label{sec:em-locality-prediction}

How wide a window does a score head need? The variance-matched Gaussian chain
answers before any network is trained, and the answer is a design
calculation for the Laplace chain, whose second-order structure it shares. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:g-posterior} the posterior precision is
$J = \Qz + (e^{-2t}/\Deltat)I$, the channel entering the diagonal alone, so
in the bulk of a long chain $J$ is tridiagonal Toeplitz,
\begin{equation}
  J = \operatorname{tridiag}(\beta,\, J_{\mathrm{d}},\, \beta),
  \qquad
  J_{\mathrm{d}} = \frac{e^{-2t}}{\Deltat} + \frac{1+\corr^2}{\ivar},
  \qquad
  \beta = -\frac{\corr}{\ivar},
  \label{eq:em-bulk-params}
\end{equation}
with only $J_{\mathrm d}$ depending on $t$.

\begin{proposition}[Bulk covariance of the chain posterior]\label{prop:em-bulk}
In the interior of a long chain,
\begin{equation}
  \big(J^{-1}\big)_{k,\,k+d} = V\,q^{|d|},
  \quad
  q = \frac{J_{\mathrm{d}} - \sqrt{J_{\mathrm{d}}^2 - 4\beta^2}}{2|\beta|} \in (0,1),
  \quad
  V = \big(J_{\mathrm{d}}^2 - 4\beta^2\big)^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:em-bulk-cov}
\end{equation}
the square root being real for every $|\corr| < 1$ and $t > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Seek $G = J^{-1}$ in the form $G_{i,i+d} = Vq^{|d|}$. For interior $i$ and
$d \ge 1$ the relation $\sum_j J_{ij}G_{jk} = \delta_{ik}$ reads
$\beta q^2 + J_{\mathrm d}q + \beta = 0$, the same quadratic for every $d$,
whose roots multiply to one, so exactly one lies inside the unit disc. The
$d = 0$ relation, $J_{\mathrm d}V + 2\beta Vq = 1$, gives $V$. Reality needs
$J_{\mathrm d} > 2|\beta|$, and
$J_{\mathrm d} - 2|\beta| = e^{-2t}/\Deltat + (1-|\corr|)^2/\ivar > 0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The formula agrees with a direct inverse at $\nsites = 32$,
$\corr = 0.85$ to $6\times10^{-15}$ at $t = 0.05$ and $2.7\times10^{-4}$ at
$t = 3$, the growth being the boundaries leaking into the bulk once the
correlation length is an appreciable fraction of the chain; its limits are
$q \to 0$ as $t \to 0$ and $q \to |\corr|$, $V \to 1$ as $t \to \infty$.
Because $m = (e^{-t}/\Deltat)J^{-1}x$ is linear in the observation, the
influence of distant evidence is exactly geometric,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial m_k}{\partial x_{k \pm d}}
  \;=\; \frac{e^{-t}}{\Deltat}\, V\, q^{\,d}
  \;=\; \frac{e^{-t} V}{\Deltat}\, e^{-d/\xi},
  \qquad
  \xi \;\coloneqq\; \frac{1}{\log(1/q)} ,
  \label{eq:em-influence}
\end{equation}
a pure exponential in the separation with a single length $\xi(\corr,t)$,
exact in the infinite-chain bulk: the object a score head has to represent,
and the closed form behind the geometric decay measured in
Section~\ref{sec:bp-locality} (RQ4). The valid risk anchor is the
Bayes-optimal radius-$r$ estimator of the design model, the windowed
posterior mean of \eqref{eq:bp-windowed}, whose population relative score
error pooled over the schedule with the same weights is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{W}(r)
  \;=\;
  \Bigg(
  \frac{\sum_{t} \operatorname{tr}\big[(M_r(t)-\Qt)\,\Sigt\,(M_r(t)-\Qt)^{\T}\big]}
       {\sum_{t} \operatorname{tr}\Qt}
  \Bigg)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:em-oracle}
\end{equation}
the best any radius-$r$ estimator can do on the covariance-matched Gaussian
model. The experiment is Laplace, whose local oracle is a different quantity
and no result ranks the Bayes risks of two priors sharing a covariance, so it
is measured by window-restricted grid BP (Table~\ref{tab:em-oracle-laplace}):
the Laplace oracle is the larger wherever the difference is resolved, and
the two are indistinguishable by $r = 8$.

\noindent The prediction follows without reference to any fit. The pooled
risk is concentrated where $\xi \lesssim 1$, so a small radius is enough where
it matters: at $t = 0.1527$ a radius of $4$ discards $q^5 = 0.9\%$ of the
influence, and the oracle error has fallen to $0.09$ at $r = 2$ and $0.03$
at $r = 4$; what remains beyond such radii comes almost entirely from the
high-noise levels, whose weight is a few percent. \emph{Prediction:} the best
window radius is small, a few sites; the risk should rise again well beyond
it, the receptive field then buying a quantity smaller than the estimation
error it costs; and the selected head should sit well above $\mathcal W(r)$
at its radius, because at small radii the window is no longer what limits a
learned head.

\subsection{The screen and the confirmatory comparison}\label{sec:em-screen}

Three candidates were screened on $\screendevseeds$ development seeds,
disjoint from the $\structseeds$ used for the comparison, each over its own
hyperparameter grid, a shared learning-rate grid, both output heads and a
shared checkpoint ladder, $\screenconfigs$ configurations in all, scored on
the \screenregion{} region. The window head won at radius
$\screenwinnerradius$ and width $\screenwinnerwidth$, the \screenrunnerup{}
network next ($\screenwinnerrisk$ against $\screenrunneruprisk$);
Table~\ref{tab:screening} and Figure~\ref{fig:em-screening} report the screen
in full. The architectures separate by parameter count far more cleanly than
by risk, and the two arms built to propagate further occupy the expensive
half of the axis without buying anything there, so the outcome is not one
arm being starved of capacity.

\emph{inside} the radius-$2$ information set, learning, capacity and
optimisation together, and it says the receptive field is not the binding
constraint at the selected radius. This is a model-selection fact under this
search space, protocol and development seeds, not evidence that long-range
propagation is useless here.

Against the screened baseline, at the headline protocol throughout, with
every site scored, the baseline's architecture and radius fixed on separate
seeds and each arm's checkpoint chosen on a disjoint validation bundle, the
surviving margin is $\structratiolo$--$\structratiohi\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:structured}, Figure~\ref{fig:em-value-of-structure}b,c). The
dotted curve in panel (b) is the two-parameter floor fit
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}(\nseq)^2 \;=\; \mathcal{E}_{\infty}^2 + \frac{c}{\nseq},
  \label{eq:em-floor}
\end{equation}
descriptive, as a four-point fit must be. EM--BP is more accurate in every
one of the $\structcells$ scored cells, a count over $\structseeds$ seeds,
$\structsizes$ sizes and $\structregions$ scoring regions that share fitted
models, so the inferential unit behind each row is the $\structseeds$ paired
seeds; under $\structaggcount$ alternative aggregations none falls below
$\structaggfloor\times$ at any size (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). A large
fraction of the headline margin therefore does not survive supplying locality
and weight sharing. The comparison does not decompose causally, since
architecture, sharing, capacity and optimisation all change when the baseline
is swapped; what it establishes is that the generic-MLP ratio overstates what
can be attributed specifically to the Markov model, by a factor that runs from
about three at the smallest training set to about $1.4$ at the largest.

\section{How much mixture capacity the estimator needs}\label{sec:em-capacity}

The estimator's one nonparametric dial is the number of mixture components.
The capacity sweep refits $C \in \{1,2,4,8,16\}$ on identical data, paired
within seed against the single-Gaussian innovation $C = 1$, at
$\nseq \in \{\capsizes\}$, and scores held-out log-evidence
(Table~\ref{tab:capacity}, Figure~\ref{fig:em-capacity}). Under this protocol
no capacity buys anything: every paired mean is negative, the
$\nseq = \capheadsize$ deficit of the largest capacity against $C = 8$ is
$\capheadverdict$ against zero ($\capheadlogev$, CI $\capheadlogevci$), and
only two to four seeds in sixteen ever prefer a mixture. The sweep is not a
clean statement about capacity in the abstract: from $C = \capcappedfrom$
upward every cell stops at the $\capiterlimit$-iteration cap rather than at
tolerance, and the share of fits whose narrowest component falls below the
grid's resolution floor climbs with $C$, so a statistical disadvantage of
large mixtures is entangled with an optimisation and a discretisation effect.
The defensible conclusion is protocol-scoped: under this grid, M-step,
iteration cap and validation rule, capacity beyond a single Gaussian
innovation bought nothing, and Chapter~\ref{ch:em-kernel} says why more
capacity is also slower. The finding bears on the headline, which runs at
$C = 8$ because the protocol fixed it in advance: a single Gaussian
innovation with a fitted $\corr$ is, by Theorem~\ref{thm:l-lmmse}, exactly the
covariance-matched linear estimator, so within the range tested the margin
over the network arms is earned by the Markov factorisation and its
covariance, not by the mixture's capacity to represent a non-Gaussian shape.

\section{Where the Markov assumption stops paying}\label{sec:em-nonmarkov}

Two controlled violations of the Markov assumption are measured against an
exact reference. \emph{A shared global latent} of strength $\beta$ adds one
standard normal draw per sequence to every site,
\begin{equation}
  a \;=\; \frac{y + \beta g}{\sqrt{1+\beta^2}},
  \qquad y \sim \text{chain prior},
  \quad g \sim \Nd(0,1),
  \label{eq:em-global}
\end{equation}
variance preserving, Markov only at $\beta = 0$, with covariance
$\Sig_\beta = (\Sigz + \beta^2\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\T)/(1+\beta^2)$, so that a
Laplace and a Gaussian version differ only beyond second moments; at
$\beta = 1$ half the marginal variance of every site is one shared constant.
\emph{Long-range precision coupling} of strength $\gamma$ adds weak couplings
directly to the precision at every separation of two or more,
\begin{equation}
  Q_\gamma = Q_{\mathrm{AR}} + \gamma\,Q_{\mathrm{long}},
  \qquad
  [Q_{\mathrm{long}}]_{ij} = -e^{-|i-j|/\ell}\ \ (|i-j| \ge 2),
  \quad
  [Q_{\mathrm{long}}]_{ii} = 1.05 \sum_{|i-j| \ge 2} e^{-|i-j|/\ell},
  \label{eq:em-longrange}
\end{equation}
with $\ell = 8$, diagonally dominant by construction and rescaled to unit
diagonal. The two mechanisms are not two amounts of the same thing.

\begin{proposition}[The global latent perturbs the chain in one direction]
\label{prop:em-rankone}
With $Q_{\mathrm{AR}} = \Sigz^{-1}$ and $Q_\beta = \Sig_\beta^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
  Q_\beta = (1+\beta^2)\,Q_{\mathrm{AR}} - c_\beta\, u u^{\T},
  \qquad
  u = Q_{\mathrm{AR}}\mathbf{1},
  \qquad
  c_\beta = \frac{(1+\beta^2)\,\beta^2}{1 + \beta^2\,\mathbf{1}^{\T}Q_{\mathrm{AR}}\mathbf{1}},
  \label{eq:em-woodbury}
\end{equation}
so the departure from a rescaled chain precision has rank one, and the lag-one
correlation of \eqref{eq:em-global}, hence that of its best first-order chain,
is $\corr_{\mathrm{CL}}(\beta) = (\corr + \beta^2)/(1+\beta^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sherman--Morrison applied to $\Sig_\beta$ gives \eqref{eq:em-woodbury}. The
covariance of \eqref{eq:em-global} at separation $d \ge 1$ is
$(\corr^d + \beta^2)/(1+\beta^2)$ with unit variance, and the KL projection of
a Gaussian onto a fixed tree matches that tree's node and edge marginals
\citep{chow1968approximating}, so on a path the fitted chain reproduces the
true adjacent correlation.
\end{proof}

\noindent Numerically the correction has one nonzero eigenvalue at every
$\beta$ tested, with eigenvector agreeing with $u/\|u\|$ to six decimals, and
$u$ is, up to boundary terms, the constant vector; the correction
$\gamma Q_{\mathrm{long}}$ is dense, with numerical rank $\nsites$. One
direction against thirty is the structural difference, and a chain that may
reparameterise a single scalar can partly trade against the first but not the
second: under \eqref{eq:em-global} the fitted $\widehat{\corr}$ climbs from
$\nmrhoclean$ to $\nmrhobetamax$ at $\beta = \nmbetamax$, tracking
$\corr_{\mathrm{CL}}$ to within $\nmcldevbeta$, whereas under
\eqref{eq:em-longrange} the same gap is $\nmcldevfactor$ times larger
(Table~\ref{tab:nonmarkov}). A single number makes the difference
quantitative, the per-site KL divergence between the covariance-matched
centred Gaussians of the contaminated law and of its best first-order chain,
\begin{equation}
  \delta \;=\; \frac{1}{\nsites}\,
  \kl{\Nd(0,\Sig)}{\Nd(0,\Sig_{\mathrm{CL}})}
  \;=\; \frac{1}{2\nsites}\Big[
  \operatorname{tr}\big(\Sig_{\mathrm{CL}}^{-1}\Sig\big) - \nsites
  + \log\frac{\det \Sig_{\mathrm{CL}}}{\det \Sig}\Big],
  \label{eq:em-delta}
\end{equation}
closed-form for both mechanisms and involving no fit. For the Gaussian arms
it is the exact KL to the best Gaussian chain; for the Laplace arms it is a
covariance proxy, blind to everything beyond second moments, which is exactly
the information the chain estimator's design geometry uses. In nats per site
it reads $4.6\times10^{-6}$, $1.5\times10^{-4}$, $1.4\times10^{-3}$ and
$6.5\times10^{-3}$ at $\beta = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1$, and $1.1\times10^{-2}$,
$2.2\times10^{-2}$, $4.0\times10^{-2}$ and $6.0\times10^{-2}$ at
$\gamma = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4$: a prior in which half of every site's
variance is shared is closer to the chain family than one with the weakest
long-range coupling tested. Table~\ref{tab:nonmarkov} and
Figure~\ref{fig:em-nonmarkov} report the measured advantage beside it.

Read against strength, the mechanisms differ in kind. At $\beta = \nmbetamax$
the Gaussian arm is still ahead at every noise level against both baselines,
worst ratio $\nmbetaminratio$, and the Laplace arm is still ahead against the
convolutional baseline, reaching break-even against the fully connected one
only at the largest diffusion time. Under long-range coupling the advantage
against the convolutional baseline is gone by $\gamma = \nmcrossover$, having
survived $\gamma = \nmlastholding$, by $\gamma = 0.2$ the estimator loses to
both baselines, and at $\gamma = \nmgammamax$ its own relative score error
reaches $\nmemworsterr\%$. Read against $\delta$, the nine contaminated cells
interleave and the advantage is monotone in $\delta$ across both mechanisms,
over four orders of magnitude in $\delta$ and a factor of twenty in the
ratio, against both baselines and for both innovation laws. The crossing is
reported as the bracket the design resolves,
\begin{equation}
  \delta^{\star} \in \big[1.1, 2.2\big]\times 10^{-2}\ \text{nats/site (vs CNN)},
  \qquad
  \big[2.2, 4.0\big]\times 10^{-2}\ \text{nats/site (vs MLP)} ;
  \label{eq:em-breakeven}
\end{equation}
each condition is a single run with no seed replication, so no interval can
be attached to a crossing and no point estimate is quoted. The collapse onto
one curve is an empirical regularity of the two violation paths constructed
here, not a sufficiency theorem: even between Gaussians equal KL does not
imply equal score geometry, since the divergence is a spectral aggregate
while the score error of a chain approximation depends on where the
precision error points, and adjacent strengths differ by roughly a factor of
two, which is the resolution of the statement.

\section{Why the shape coordinate is the expensive one}
\label{sec:em-shape-information}

Chapter~\ref{ch:em-kernel} attributed the slow convergence of the innovation
shape to the fourth cumulant being damped by the channel while the second is
preserved. That is a claim about the noised likelihood, and it can be checked
against Fisher information independently of any optimiser. For
$X_t = e^{-t}A + \sqrt{\Deltat}\,\varepsilon$, cumulants add under
independent sums and the Gaussian contributes only to the second, so
$\kappa_2(X_t) = 1$, $\kappa_4(X_t) = e^{-4t}\kappa_4(A)$ and
$\kappa_{11}(X_{t,i}, X_{t,i+1}) = e^{-2t}\corr$: the autoregressive
coefficient, identified through the lag-one cumulant, is attenuated by
$e^{-2t}$, a shape parameter moving the fourth cumulant at fixed variance by
$e^{-4t}$. Reading information as squared sensitivity over a sampling
variance that is $t$-independent to leading order because
$\kappa_2(X_t) \equiv 1$ pins the scale, a delta-method heuristic rather than
a likelihood calculation,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  I_{\corr}(t) \;\asymp\; e^{-4t},
  \qquad
  I_{p}(t) \;\asymp\; e^{-8t},
  \qquad
  \frac{I_{p}(t)}{I_{\corr}(t)} \;\asymp\; e^{-4t},
  \;}
  \label{eq:em-info-prediction}
\end{equation}
and the third statement is the robust one, a contrast from which common
omitted factors cancel.

The prediction is tested on generalised Gaussian innovations
$f_p(\eta) \propto \exp(-|\eta/s|^p)$ with $s$ fixed by
$\operatorname{Var}(\eta) = \ivar$ at every $p$, so that $p$ is a genuine
shape coordinate ($p = 1$ Laplace, $p = 2$ Gaussian). For
$\theta = (\corr, \ivar, p)$, Fisher's identity \eqref{eq:em-fisher} supplies
the exact per-sequence score of the marginal log-likelihood from one
forward--backward pass, the information matrix is estimated in outer-product
form over $D = 512$ sequences of $\nsites = 32$ sites, and the quantity
reported is the \emph{efficient} information
$I_{p\cdot(\corr,\ivar)} = 1/(J^{-1})_{pp}$, what remains about $p$ once the
nuisance coordinates must be estimated too. Two caveats travel with every
number: an outer-product matrix is the Fisher information only at the true
parameter of a correctly specified model and is here a $D$-sample estimate;
and the reported quantity is an inverse whose meaning degrades with the
conditioning of $J$, benign where the information is large
($\kappa(J) = \infcondbest$ at $t = \infcondbestt$, median $\infcondmedian$)
and failing where it vanishes ($\infcondworst$ in the worst cell), so the far
tail says the information has gone, not how much remains.
Figure~\ref{fig:em-information} and Table~\ref{tab:em-information} report
the result: both measured decays are shallower than predicted by a common
offset, and the contrast, which the offset cancels, brackets the prediction
with a mean fitted log-slope of $-3.72$ against $-4$ and a geometric-mean
information loss of $465$ against a predicted $493$.

\section{What the chapter establishes}\label{sec:em-summary}

Four findings, each at the strength its evidence supports. Supplying the
chain is worth $\ratiolo$--$\ratiohi\times$ lower pooled error over
\nsizesused{} sizes against no structure, under the schedule-pooled paired
estimand with both arms budget-selected on validation. Against a baseline
given locality and weight sharing the margin is
$\structratiolo$--$\structratiohi\times$, EM--BP ahead in every scored cell,
for one screened architecture and four sizes, with no causal split of the
gap; most of the headline factor does not survive, which bounds how much of
it can be attributed specifically to the Markov model. No tested mixture
capacity beats a single Gaussian innovation under this grid, M-step,
iteration cap and validation rule. And across the tested violations the
advantage ordered monotonically with a covariance scalar computable before
any fitting, with break-even bracketed between adjacent strengths, an
empirical regularity of nine cells and two mechanisms, useful as a screening
variable and not established as a decision rule. Throughout, the coordinate
on which every non-Gaussian claim depends is simultaneously the slowest to
converge and the poorest in information per observation, which is why the
budget disclosures in this chapter are part of its results.
