\chapter{Learning the Kernel, and What Convergence Costs}\label{ch:em-kernel}

Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method} established that the estimator is correct: the
E-step is exact, the surrogate ascends, and the recovery error of the
second-order coordinates falls approximately as $\nseq^{-1/2}$ over the
tested range. None of that says how long it takes. Correctness is a statement
about the fixed point; this chapter is about the map that reaches it, and the
two are independent: an estimator can be exactly right in the limit and still
be routinely reported before it arrives. The finding is that the map does not
converge at one speed. The autoregressive coefficient and the innovation
\emph{shape} contract at rates that differ by a factor of eight in the
exponent, so a stopping rule calibrated on the first certifies a kernel that
has not converged in the second, and every non-Gaussian claim in this thesis
depends on the coordinate that settles last.

\section{Why there are two rates}\label{sec:em-rate-theory}\label{sec:em-two-effects}\label{sec:em-which-slower}

The M-step defines a map $\theta^{(k+1)} = \mathcal M(\theta^{(k)})$ whose
fixed points are stationary points $\theta^\star$ of the marginal likelihood,
and EM converges linearly, at a rate set by the spectrum of the Jacobian of
that map at the fixed point, which \citet{dempster1977maximum} identified.
Expanding about $\theta^\star$,
\begin{equation}
  \theta^{(k+1)} - \theta^\star
  \;=\; J(\theta^\star)\,\big(\theta^{(k)} - \theta^\star\big)
     \;+\; O\big(\|\theta^{(k)} - \theta^\star\|^2\big),
  \qquad
  J \;=\; \frac{\partial \mathcal M}{\partial \theta}\bigg|_{\theta^\star}.
  \label{eq:em-fixedpoint}
\end{equation}
With
$I_{\mathrm{com}} = -\E[\nabla^2\log p_\theta(a,c,x)\mid x]$ the expected
complete-data information given $x$, $I_{\mathrm{obs}} = -\nabla^2\mathcal L$
the observed information and $I_{\mathrm{mis}}$ their difference, all at
$\theta^\star$,
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mathrm{com}} \;=\; I_{\mathrm{obs}} \;+\; I_{\mathrm{mis}},
  \qquad
  J(\theta^\star)
  \;=\; I_{\mathrm{com}}^{-1} I_{\mathrm{mis}}
  \;=\; \mathbb{I} - I_{\mathrm{com}}^{-1} I_{\mathrm{obs}} :
  \label{eq:em-missing-info}
\end{equation}
the rate of EM \emph{is} the fraction of the information that the latent
variables carry away: as $I_{\mathrm{mis}} \to I_{\mathrm{com}}$ the largest
eigenvalue of $J$ approaches one and progress per update approaches nothing.
One scope condition governs everything measured below. Identity
\eqref{eq:em-missing-info} is the Jacobian of \emph{exact} EM; the mixture
M-step of Chapter~\ref{ch:em-method} cycles conditional maximisations with at
most sixteen inner sweeps, and with $r$ sweeps its Jacobian is
$G^{r} + (\mathbb{I} - G^{r})J$, $G$ being the block Gauss--Seidel matrix of
those maximisations. So \eqref{eq:em-missing-info} is an ordering heuristic
for which coordinate should be slow, never a calibrated prediction of how
slow.

A coordinate aligned with an eigenvector of $J$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$
contracts as $\lambda^k$, so stopping it when its relative distance to the
limit first falls below a tolerance $\tau$ takes
\begin{equation}
  k(\tau, \lambda) \;\approx\; \frac{\log \tau}{\log \lambda},
  \qquad
  \frac{k_2}{k_1}
  \;\approx\;
  \frac{\log \tau_2}{\log \tau_1}
  \cdot
  \frac{\log \lambda_1}{\log \lambda_2} ,
  \label{eq:em-settle}
\end{equation}
which separates a choice of tolerance from a property of the model. Two
limits are stated now and verified below: it is an asymptotic-tail statement,
a trace can fall much faster than its terminal slope during the transient, so
\eqref{eq:em-settle} upper-bounds a measured settling time; and it speaks
about eigenmodes, whereas a parameter coordinate mixes several, so a slope
fitted to a coordinate's trace is an empirical contraction factor, identified
with an eigenvalue only if one mode dominates its window.

The observation channel enters \eqref{eq:em-missing-info} twice. \emph{On the
rate:} fitting on clean transition pairs leaves only the mixture label
latent; passing the chains through the channel makes the whole state $a$
latent as well, so $I_{\mathrm{mis}}$ grows by everything the posterior over
$a$ does not resolve, $J$ moves towards the identity, and the map slows, a
statement about iterations that would persist at infinite sample size.
\emph{On the accuracy:} the channel also shrinks $I_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and the
asymptotic covariance of the estimator is $I_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}/\nseq$, a
statement about data that iterating longer does not remove.
Section~\ref{sec:em-information} measures it.

Which coordinate should be slower the model says before any fit is run. The
channel is variance preserving and the chain is normalised to unit marginal
variance, so the second cumulant of the noised variable is preserved exactly
at every $t$ while the fourth is damped,
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_2(X_t) \;=\; \kappa_2(A) \quad \text{for all } t,
  \qquad
  \kappa_4(X_t) \;=\; e^{-4t}\,\kappa_4(A)
  \label{eq:em-cumulant-damping}
\end{equation}
\eqref{eq:l-state-kurtosis}. The autoregressive coefficient is a second-order
quantity, identified by the lag-one covariance, which the channel attenuates
in a known, invertible way; the innovation shape is a fourth-order quantity,
and the channel leaves only an $e^{-4t}$ fraction of exactly the structure that
identifies it. The information the channel takes away is concentrated on the
shape coordinate, which by \eqref{eq:em-missing-info} is the coordinate whose
eigenvalue of $J$ is pushed closest to one. \emph{Prediction:}
$\lambda_{\kappa_4} > \lambda_{\alpha}$, and by \eqref{eq:em-settle} a
settling time for the shape that is a large multiple of the settling time for
the correlation.

\section{Measuring the two rates}\label{sec:em-two-rates}

A dedicated sweep fits the mixture kernel through the channel at
$\nseq = \convsize$, $C = \convcomps$ and the \convdesign{} noise design, over
$\convseeds$ seeds run to $\convcap$ updates, recording the full parameter
trace. Figure~\ref{fig:em-two-rates}(a) plots each coordinate's relative
distance to its own end-of-run value: both are straight on a log axis over a
wide window, which is \eqref{eq:em-fixedpoint} being obeyed, and the slopes
differ sharply (Table~\ref{tab:em-rates}).

The prediction is confirmed in direction and in magnitude of effect. The
shape contracts by $0.9960$ per update against $0.9686$ for the correlation,
a factor of $8.0$ in the exponent, and the innovation \emph{variance} settles
with the correlation at $0.9671$, the same statement from the other side: it
is a second-order quantity and the channel preserves it. Read through
\eqref{eq:em-missing-info} this ordering is consistent with a slow,
shape-dominated eigenmode whose missing information sits far closer to one
than the correlation's; consistent with, not a measurement of, since turning
slopes into eigenvalues would require the Jacobian of the implemented map,
which is not computed. Where the linear theory is not quantitative is the
constant: \eqref{eq:em-settle} predicts a settling ratio of $4.5$, the paired
measurement gives $\convratio$, and the asymptotic counts over-predict the
correlation's settling time by about three and the shape's by about four,
because both curves
fall faster in their first hundred updates than their fitted slopes, so both
tolerances are crossed in the transient rather than in the asymptotic regime.
The eigenvalues bound the tail, not the crossing of a threshold.

\section{Settling times and the stopping rule}\label{sec:em-stopping-times}

Across $\convseeds$ seeds the correlation settles at a median of
$\convrhomed$ updates and the shape at $\convshapemed$, the largest observed
value being $\convshapemax$ (Figure~\ref{fig:em-two-rates}(b)); the ratio
formed within each fit has median $\convratio$, against $\convratioofmedians$
for the quotient of medians, and the paired figure is the one reported.
``Settled'' means trace stability, the first update after which a coordinate
stays within a relative $10^{-3}$ of its end-of-run value for $\alpha$ or a
relative $2\times10^{-2}$ for the excess kurtosis, not distance to an
optimiser, and
the medians come from the $\convcap$-update shard, the only run length at
which no configuration settles near its own cap. Against them, the fixed
budget of $\convbudget$ iterations that several experiments in
Chapter~\ref{ch:em-results} used as a default falls short of the shape's
median by about a factor of six and leaves $\convunsettled$ of the sweep's
$\convunsettledof$ configurations, $\convunsettledpct\%$, unsettled, a count
that pools both shards and is conservative because censoring can only shorten
a measured settling time. Table~\ref{tab:em-convergence} makes the same point
on one configuration: the correlation is essentially in place by thirty
updates while the innovation shape is still climbing at a hundred and twenty.
The mixture label is a latent variable of its own and the channel compounds
the missing information, the mechanism of \citet{dempster1977maximum}
appearing in the convergence rate exactly where \eqref{eq:em-missing-info}
places it.

\section{What the channel costs in accuracy at equal data}
\label{sec:em-information}

The endpoints in Table~\ref{tab:em-convergence} are close: the two arms finish
within $0.002$ of each other on the correlation and within about a fifth of a
unit on excess kurtosis, both near the generating values. Under a correctly
specified, identifiable model the clean and noised likelihoods share their
population maximiser and shared endpoints are what shrinking
$I_{\mathrm{obs}}$ predicts; the fitted family here is a finite mixture that
does not contain the Laplace innovation exactly, and under misspecification
the two pseudo-true optima need not coincide, so the close endpoints are a
measured approximate invariance for this family in this regime, not a
theorem. Either way they are not evidence that the channel is free: the
second effect lives in the variance, and Table~\ref{tab:em-channel-cost}
measures it on the quantity the thesis uses, the relative score error of the
fitted kernel at $t = 0.2$.

The penalty is real at every size and over the measured range it grows: the
clean arm improves fourfold, close to the $\nseq^{-1/2}$ behaviour of
Figure~\ref{fig:em-diagnostics}(c), while the noised arm improves only
$2.6$-fold and is nearly flat between $\nseq = 512$ and $1024$, so the ratio
moves from $2.0\times$ to $3.0\times$. Under a regular, correctly specified
model both arms obey $\sqrt{\nseq}\,(\widehat\theta - \theta^\star)
\Rightarrow \Nd(0, I_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1})$, so a smaller observed information
enlarges the constant of an $\nseq^{-1/2}$ law and cannot by itself produce
a plateau; a genuine flattening needs something else, the finite-mixture
approximation floor, quadrature and stopping tolerances, a shift of the
noised pseudo-true target under misspecification, or a pre-asymptotic range,
and three replicates over five sizes cannot separate these. What the table
establishes is a larger finite-data cost through the channel, widening over
the tested range, not a changed asymptotic rate; reduced $I_{\mathrm{obs}}$
remains the mechanism for the level of the penalty, and equal endpoints with
unequal precision are the two halves of one statement.
